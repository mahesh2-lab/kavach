How Often Is a Fire Safety Audit Legally Required in Maharashtra?

Home / Blog / Article

For industrial plant owners, warehouse operators, and commercial facility managers operating in Maharashtra, maintaining statutory fire safety compliance is a legal necessity. Under the Maharashtra Fire Prevention and Life Safety Measures Act 2006 and the National Building Code (NBC) of India 2016 Part 4, industrial units must undergo regular fire audits and submit maintenance certifications to local fire authorities.

Failing to adhere to mandated audit schedules leaves businesses vulnerable to severe consequences: immediate cancellation of operational licenses, rejection of property insurance claims following a fire incident, and direct prosecution of factory management by municipal fire departments and the Directorate of Industrial Safety and Health (DISH).

The Bi-Annual Mandate: Form B Certification

A common misconception among factory managers is that an initial Fire No Objection Certificate (Fire NOC) provides permanent compliance. In reality, the Maharashtra Fire Act explicitly mandates that every industrial establishment must submit an operational certificate, known as Form B, twice every calendar year:

  • January Submission Window: Form B certifying full operational readiness for the first half of the year.
  • July Submission Window: Form B certifying full operational readiness for the second half of the year.

Form B can only be issued by a certified Licensed Agency approved by the Maharashtra Fire Services. The agency must conduct a thorough physical audit of on-site industrial fire hydrant systems, automatic sprinklers, smoke detectors, and manual extinguishers before certifying that the equipment is maintained in working order.

Comprehensive Third-Party Safety Audits under IS 14489

Beyond bi-annual Form B renewals, manufacturing plants categorized as Moderate or High Hazard under the Factories Act 1948 must conduct a comprehensive, independent certified fire safety audit every two years in accordance with IS 14489 ("Code of Practice on Occupational Safety and Health Audit").

An IS 14489 audit goes deeper than basic hardware checks. Certified safety auditors evaluate chemical storage containment, electrical thermographic hotspots, emergency exit travel distances, fire compartmentation barriers, and the emergency readiness of the plant Emergency Response Team (ERT).

Key Triggers for Unscheduled Fire Audits

In addition to regular statutory cycles, industrial facilities must immediately commission a fresh fire safety audit when any of the following triggers occur:

  • Facility Expansion or Layout Modification: Adding new mezzanine floors, expanding warehouse footprints, or altering pallet rack configurations.
  • Process or Chemical Hazard Changes: Introducing new flammable solvents, Class I combustible liquids, or high-density battery energy storage systems (BESS).
  • Post-Incident Investigations: Following any localized fire, electrical explosion, or near-miss incident to identify root causes and remediate system deficiencies.
